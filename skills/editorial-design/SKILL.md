# Editorial Design

Choose a visual because it makes a particular idea clearer, more felt, or more useful to its
audience. A calendar is a source of candidates, not a limit on what deserves attention.
Start with the current message, source material and relevant human edits, not a fixed template.

## Find the visual argument

Read the actual source and inspect the pixels or footage. Identify what the audience should
notice and why that matters. Select an expressive form: a real photograph, focused screenshot,
annotated detail, illustrated idea, sequence, filmed process, or motion demonstration. Text alone
is valid when a visual adds nothing. Do not force every subject into a carousel or product ad.

Separate observed reality, authored illustration, composited presentation and hypothetical scene.
Never use generated pixels as proof of product behavior, a user testimonial or a real event.
Record source ownership and permitted use; a public image is not automatically reusable.

## Produce with the available tools

Use a capability suited to the job and read its instructions. Image generation/editing belongs
to the available image tool; editable layout belongs to a design tool; trimming, captions and
encoding belong to the available video workflow. A skill is not evidence that a tool has run.
Do not acquire subscriptions or install a new service just to fulfill this method.

For images, choose composition, hierarchy, crop, typography, colour and whitespace around the
actual point. Preserve recognizable product details and readable text. Inspect the exported
image at its expected viewing size, including mobile; checking source code is insufficient.

For videos, start with a concise shot sequence: opening reason to watch, the action or evidence,
and a useful ending. Use real continuous footage where the claim is a real workflow. Label a
slideshow or reconstruction accurately. Check the complete exported duration, transitions,
text timing, captions, end frame and audio when present; sampled frames alone do not prove
audio quality or continuity. Do not silently substitute stills when motion is essential.

Use source assets and editable project files where practical. Keep the exact export hash,
dimensions, duration, caption/alt text, and the relevant copy version together on the original
content card. Verify that the review surface actually displays or plays this export. A local
file path, upload response or storyboard does not establish delivered media.

## Revise and learn

Read [feedback and review](references/feedback.md) when a user edits, comments, approves or lets
a review window pass. Inspect the before and after artifacts, not only a textual summary.
Keep project-specific taste, examples and source material in the project's private records.
Retrieve the few relevant corrections before the next related design and show how they were
applied. Preserve creative latitude outside their stated scope.

Never promise first-pass satisfaction. Report whether production, export inspection, delivery,
human preference confirmation and audience outcomes were actually observed. Better work is
demonstrated by the next artifact and feedback, not a self-assigned quality score.

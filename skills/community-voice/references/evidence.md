# Evidence and feedback

Keep a small project-owned profile, not a second publishing database.

An evidence item needs an ID, source locator and observed version/hash, author/evidence type,
relevant excerpt or bounded summary, applicable speaker/task/language, allowed use, known
context gaps and any superseding instruction. Do not publish private excerpts with this skill.

Distinguish:
- Human instruction: authority only within its stated scope; apply a clear correction now.
- Human-authored example: evidence of that speaker in that situation, not universal style.
- Explicitly approved assisted output: retain assistance and exact approval scope.
- Recommended third-party example: evidence of an admired conversational choice, not the
  speaker's personal voice or permission to reuse the text or train a model.
- Operator output: a draft or execution example; publication and editor acceptance alone
  do not turn it into a human-endorsed preference.
- Audience observation: an actual reaction with date and context; not causal proof or taste
  approval. A thank-you can acknowledge help without consenting to an invitation.

When signals conflict, current scoped human instruction controls the requested expression.
An older example can still be useful outside that scope. Explain concrete conflicts; do not
average all sources into a vague voice. Current facts and explicit commitments constrain copy.

For feedback, preserve the relevant first draft and final text, exact correction, reason
(misunderstanding, factual error, wrong voice, generic phrasing, duplication, pressure), scope,
and next eligible use. Validate that use with its actual input/output and subsequent result.
No automatic training, global ban or new follow-up schedule follows from saving an example.

For comparison, use new source groups and the same factual brief. Retain the baseline and
candidate's first outputs and disclose reviewer identity. Report direct adoption, minor edits,
substantial rewrite, or blocked, with reasons; do not silently exclude failures. An author
walkthrough is a development check, not an independent benchmark. Missing human judgments
cannot be replaced by model scores. No fixed success percentage is implied by this method.
